# WTF is this project?!

The Nokia N810 is the kind of tech trash that's hard to throw away. Slide the screen up and there's a real keyboard. Inside there's an ARM processor, 128 MB of RAM and a graphics chip. What would it take to use the thing as a little Linux computer again?

That's how you end up with Debian on a Nokia internet tablet and Pocket, a desktop built for it. You can open a map on the tablet, play sound through its speakers, or connect a handheld radio. You can also run Pocket in a terminal on your laptop, which is a much more convenient place to figure out why a window won't fit on an 800 × 480 screen.

Part of the excuse is getting to tinker with AI agents. Give an agent a kernel tree, a set of old board files and a boot log, and there's plenty for it to dig through. The fun part is finding out what happens when you try the result. On one tablet, a new system came up with a blank screen but still answered over USB. On another, the same SD card that kept timing out booted promptly after we swapped the adapter. You can spend a long time reading code before you think to try a different piece of plastic. [Read the installation story](posts/2026-11-17-pocket-on-the-second-n810.md).

Some detours earn their keep. Getting the old graphics chip to draw a chessboard is a fine excuse to play a move and see whether a Symbian game will answer. If you're here for that kind of thing, [start with the journal](journal.md). If you've got an N810 in a drawer, [see what works on the hardware](status.md). And if you're just curious what Pocket feels like, [try it on your laptop](tutorials/first-session.md).
